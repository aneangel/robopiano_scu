# Paper Figures — What Each One Shows and the Caveats

Generated by `benchmark/figures/make_paper_figures.py`. Re-run any time the
CSVs under `benchmark/results/eval_runs/` are updated. All figures are PDF
for LaTeX inclusion.

## Apples-to-apples within our own work (figures 1-5)

These compare configurations of OUR method on the SAME songs under the SAME
evaluation conditions (30 s active window, rollout F1 via
`retest_impromptu_rp1m_simulator.py`). Use these as the primary evidence.

### `fig1_f1_histogram_177.pdf`
Per-song rollout-F1 distribution on all 177 MAESTRO v3 test-split songs.
Overlay of the *Baseline* config (`fa853b9`, no multistart, nfev=60) and the
*F1-lift* config (`cad9c3a`, multistart=6, nfev=240). Vertical red line at
the 0.60 floor. **Apples-to-apples: same 177 songs, same eval window, same
rollout pipeline.** Numbers in legend: baseline 0.580 mean, lift 0.605 mean.

### `fig2_f1_cdf_177.pdf`
"Fraction of songs with F1 ≥ x" curves for the same two configs. Reads
left-to-right: how many of the 177 songs each method clears each F1 bar.
PianoMime / OmniPianist / PANDORA reported F1s are drawn as dotted
reference lines for visual context (NOT a head-to-head).

### `fig3_f1_vs_duration.pdf`
Scatter of per-song F1 vs music duration on the 177 songs, F1-lift config.
Red line = bin mean per duration bucket. **Headline finding:** F1 degrades
with song length (≥500 s songs average ~0.55, short etudes ≥0.65). This
motivates the duration-aware future-work paragraph in §5.

### `fig4_ablation.pdf`
Cumulative architectural ablation. Each bar = mean F1 after adding that
lever on top of the prior. Right-axis red line = wall time. Shows that
F1 from 0.369 → 0.778 on the synthetic dense_test came from adding the
keyset cache, analytical Jacobian, and avoid_mispresses + topk routing —
**not** from any single change. Wall drops from 119 s to 22 s along the
same chain. *Caveat:* this is the synthetic stress MIDI, not MAESTRO;
the F1 ceiling on real songs is lower (see fig 1).

### `fig5_speed_quality_pareto.pdf`
Mean wall vs mean F1 for every measured configuration. Point area scales
with subset size (1 song dense_test → 177 songs full split). Vertical
dashed lines at 60 s ideal and 120 s budget; horizontal dashed at the
0.60 floor. Shows the F1-lift config moves up-and-right (better F1 at
the cost of wall) and that synthetic dense_test sits in an entirely
different regime from real MAESTRO songs.

## Cross-method comparison (figure 6, with caveats)

### `fig6_baseline_comparison.pdf`
Bar chart of reported rollout F1 across PianoMime, OmniPianist, PANDORA,
and our three measured variants. **NOT apples-to-apples in evaluation
conditions:**
  - PianoMime: ~0.56 reported on "novel" pieces (different sampling, not MAESTRO test split)
  - OmniPianist: ~0.55 reported on 100 unseen pieces
  - PANDORA: ~0.65 reported on **Etude-12** (a curated 12-song subset, not MAESTRO)
  - Our 0.580 / 0.605 / 0.631 are all on the same MAESTRO v3 test split

The plot caption explicitly notes this. Use it as a *reference frame*
("our method is in the same ballpark as published learned baselines"),
not as a head-to-head ("we beat them by X"). The paper text should
acknowledge that a direct re-implementation of any baseline on
MAESTRO v3 test split is future work.

## Honest qualifiers you must keep in the paper text

1. **Eval methodology**: We use a 30 s active window on each MAESTRO song, NOT the full song. Per-song wall ≤ 2 min is hit at this window; full-song eval breaks the wall budget. We test on the first 30 s but call this 30 s a "windowed evaluation" in the text — do not call it full-song.

2. **Baseline comparison**: PianoMime, PANDORA, OmniPianist evaluate on different subsets / metrics. Our numbers are not a direct head-to-head. Frame as "our method lands in the same operating range as published learned baselines while remaining fully interpretable."

3. **F1 floor**: 0.60 on the 177 test split with the F1-lift config. Mean is 0.605 — clears the floor by 0.005, so the margin is honest but small. The variance is meaningful (median 0.610, p25 ~0.52, p75 ~0.68); the gain is broad across songs but the variance reflects that some pieces (Liszt transcriptions, late Romantic sonatas) remain hard.

4. **1276-song result not in figures**: We have not yet run the full MAESTRO v3 (1276 songs). Figure 1 and 2 are the 177 test split only. If we run 1276 before submission, update fig1/fig2 to show 1276 distribution; the script accepts the new CSV at `benchmark/results/eval_runs/maestro_1276.csv` and will re-plot.
